Selling a Condo in Palm Beach County

What owners should know under Florida’s condo safety rules

Condos are a big part of the Palm Beach County market, from oceanfront towers to garden-style communities near the Turnpike. Since Florida tightened its condo safety rules, selling a condo takes more preparation than it used to. Buyers, lenders and insurers now look closely at the building, not just the unit. Here is how condo owners can get ready.

Know where your building stands

Older buildings three stories and taller must complete milestone structural inspections, and associations must fund structural reserves based on a reserve study. Before listing, ask your association for:

  • The latest milestone inspection report, if your building needs one
  • The structural integrity reserve study and current reserve balances
  • The annual budget, recent board minutes and any planned special assessments
  • The master insurance policy and any pending litigation

Buyers will receive many of these documents during their review period. Knowing what is in them ahead of time lets you price and answer questions with confidence.

Special assessments change the math

If your building has a large assessment coming, buyers will factor it into their offers. Some sellers pay the assessment before closing, others credit the buyer, and some price the unit to reflect it. A local agent can show what similar units in assessed buildings have sold for.

Financing matters more than ever

Some lenders and loan programs will not finance units in buildings with low reserves, ongoing litigation or incomplete inspections. If your building has these issues, cash buyers may be your strongest pool, which affects both price and timing.

Price the unit against the right sales

A unit in a building with completed repairs and funded reserves can sell for noticeably more than a similar unit next door. Start with a free condo value estimate for Palm Beach County and ask which buildings the comparable sales came from.
