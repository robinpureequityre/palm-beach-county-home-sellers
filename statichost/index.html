How to Price Your Palm Beach County Home

A seller’s guide to comparable sales from Pure Equity Realty US

Pricing is the single biggest decision a seller makes. Price too high and the home sits while buyers move on to fresher listings. Price too low and you leave money behind. In Palm Beach County, where a condo, a villa in a 55+ community and a pool home can share a zip code, getting the comparison right is harder than it looks. Here is how we approach it.

Comparable sales, not asking prices

Active listings show what sellers hope to get. Closed sales show what buyers actually paid. A solid price starts with homes that sold in the last three to six months, in the same community or the same type of neighborhood, with similar size, age and condition.

Adjusting for what Florida buyers pay for

  • Roof age. A newer roof lowers the buyer's insurance cost, which raises what they can afford.
  • Impact windows and doors. These add value and help with insurance discounts.
  • Pool and outdoor space. In many neighborhoods a pool is expected, not a bonus.
  • HOA and club costs. High monthly fees reduce what buyers will pay for the home itself.
  • Flood zone. Homes requiring flood insurance often sell for less than similar homes outside the zone.

Why online estimates miss

Automated values rarely know about a new roof, a renovated kitchen or a premium lot backing onto a lake or golf course. They also struggle in communities with few recent sales. That is why we recommend starting with a home value estimate reviewed by a local agent, then adjusting it once someone has seen the home.

The first two weeks decide a lot

New listings get the most attention in their first ten to fourteen days. A home priced right for its market tends to get its best offers in that window. One priced high usually needs a reduction later and often sells for less than if it had been priced right from the start.

Pricing differs by city
